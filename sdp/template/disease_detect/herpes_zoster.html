What is Herpes Zoster?

Herpes zoster, also known as shingles, is a viral infection caused by the varicella-zoster virus, which also causes chickenpox. The virus can remain dormant in the nervous system after a person recovers from chickenpox, and can reactivate later in life to cause shingles. Shingles typically presents as a painful rash that develops on one side of the body, usually on the trunk or face.

Symptoms

The symptoms of herpes zoster usually begin with a burning or tingling sensation, followed by the development of a painful rash. The rash typically appears as a band or strip of blisters on one side of the body, and may be accompanied by fever, headache, and fatigue. The rash may take several weeks to heal, and in some cases, can cause long-term complications such as nerve damage or chronic pain.


When to See a Doctor

If you suspect you have shingles, it is important to see a healthcare provider for an accurate diagnosis and treatment plan. Early treatment with antiviral medications can help reduce the severity and duration of symptoms.

How to Treat Herpes Zoster

Treatment for shingles typically involves antiviral medications, pain relievers, and topical creams or ointments to relieve itching and discomfort. In some cases, oral or injectable steroids may be used to reduce inflammation and swelling. It is important to avoid scratching or picking at the rash, as this can lead to further complications such as bacterial infections. Vaccination against herpes zoster is also available to help prevent the development of shingles in people who have previously had chickenpox.
